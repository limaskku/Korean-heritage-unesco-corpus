# Constructing Korean Heritage: A Corpus of UNESCO World Heritage Descriptions

## Research Question

How is Korean cultural heritage represented through recurring concepts of tradition, identity, preservation, religion, and history in UNESCO World Heritage descriptions?

## Corpus Description

This archive contains six English-language descriptions of cultural heritage properties in the Republic of Korea listed by the UNESCO World Heritage Centre.

The corpus includes:

- Changdeokgung Palace Complex
- Gyeongju Historic Areas
- Hwaseong Fortress
- Jongmyo Shrine
- Royal Tombs of the Joseon Dynasty
- Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple

Each heritage property is represented as a separate text file.

## Selection Criteria

The texts were selected from the official UNESCO World Heritage Centre website.

The corpus includes different types of Korean cultural heritage, including palaces, historic areas, fortresses, shrines, royal tombs, and Buddhist heritage.

This variety makes it possible to compare how different forms of cultural heritage are described within the same institutional source.

## Data Organization

The archive is organized at the document level. Each heritage property corresponds to one individual `.txt` file.

The archive also contains:

- `metadata.csv` — information about each heritage property, including its type, historical period, location, and inscription year.
- `sources.txt` — the original UNESCO sources for each text.
- `README.md` — documentation explaining the purpose, organization, and methodology of the archive.

## Source

All texts were collected from the official UNESCO World Heritage Centre.

The original source links are provided in `sources.txt`.

## Method

The textual corpus is analyzed using Voyant Tools.

The analysis focuses on:

1. Word frequency
2. Distribution of terms across documents
3. Trends in recurring vocabulary
4. Contexts in which important terms appear
5. Collocations between important concepts
6. Differences between descriptions of different types of heritage

Particular attention is given to vocabulary related to tradition, history, cultural identity, preservation, and religion.

## Purpose

The purpose of this archive is to explore how computational text analysis can reveal recurring patterns in institutional descriptions of Korean cultural heritage.

The project combines quantitative analysis of word frequency and distribution with qualitative interpretation of textual contexts.

## Limitations

This corpus represents UNESCO's institutional descriptions of Korean cultural heritage.

It should not be interpreted as a representation of how Korean society as a whole understands or defines cultural heritage.

The analysis therefore focuses specifically on the language and discourse used by UNESCO to describe these heritage properties.
